# Healing Soil: honest viability review and 15-month direction

**Date:** 2026-10-10

## Context
Sai asked if Healing Soil is a viable business and why it feels like a constant game of catching up on profit. The bar they set is **full-time income (₹75k+/month)**. Sources:
- Every order and expense in SoapLedger from Nov 2025 to 10 Oct 2026, pulled with read-only queries.
- Sai's answers: under 8 hrs/week, under 100 bars/month capacity, WhatsApp only, no GST or cosmetic licence, the next materials order is under ₹5k, and nobody has decided to go full-time.
- Research on other solo makers.

## The numbers (rupees, all 11 months)
| | |
|---|---|
| Revenue | **₹82,056**: 75 orders from 55 customers, about ₹7.5k a month |
| Everything spent | **₹80,005** |
| Net so far | **about +₹2,000**, so roughly break-even |
| Excluding one-time costs (equipment ₹14k, trademark ₹4.7k, courses ₹3.3k) | about +₹24k, or **₹2,300 a month** |
| Materials + packaging per bar sold | about ₹115 against an average price of about ₹290, so **about 60% gross margin** |
| Your time | about 26–32 hrs/month, so **₹70–90 an hour**, around unskilled minimum wage. Paying yourselves even ₹200/hr would mean a loss of about ₹3k a month |

Months: Dec 4.5k · Jan 1.9k · Feb 7.5k · Mar 10.5k · Apr 7.6k · May 16.3k (₹8.8k of it from one family) · Jun 3.0k · Jul 7.0k · Aug 5.2k · Sep 15.2k (best real month). The line is **volatile and mostly flat**. There's no compounding yet.

## Why it feels like catching up
The cause is **not** the raw-material order. At under ₹5k it's normal restocking, and a 60% margin covers it easily.
- **The business is too small to absorb experiments.** Monthly profit is about ₹2–3k, so any one-off purchase eats one to three months of it.
- **Bets are taking that profit.** About **₹37k of the ₹80k (46%)** went on things that weren't soap ingredients or packaging:
  - equipment ₹14k
  - ads about ₹9.3k, which brought in roughly 4 trackable orders worth about ₹3–5k, so ads lost money
  - trademark ₹4.7k
  - an influencer and hampers ₹4.6k
  - courses ₹3.3k
  - stall ₹2.5k
- Each bet was reasonable on its own. Together they explain why profit never piles up.

## What you're doing right
1. **Price and margin are healthy.** About 60% gross margin on premium prices (₹250–400) that people pay. Pricing is not the problem.
2. **People who try it come back.** 10 of 55 customers reordered, and they bring in **44% of revenue**. One customer ordered 7 times. This is the strongest signal in the data.
3. **Sales come from word of mouth.** Referral and WhatsApp customers cost almost nothing to win.
4. **You actually know your numbers.** Most makers never do; the forum threads are full of people who don't.

## What you're doing wrong
1. **There's no reliable way to reach new buyers.** It's personal WhatsApp only. 40 of 75 orders have no recorded source, so you can't learn what works.
2. **Effort goes into the tools more than the business.** SoapLedger has 323 commits, a growth-insights AI, a reel builder and a SaaS plan, while the soap side gets under 8 hrs/week and earns ₹7.5k a month. The software is more mature than the business it serves. If the goal is full-time income from soap, this split is the biggest problem.
3. **Too many products: 29 for about 7 orders a month.** That means scattered stock, more base and oil types to buy, and lumpier purchases. Six products bring in about half of revenue.
4. **A ceiling from the rules (revised after Sai's pushback).**
   - **Cosmetic licence (COS-8):** out of reach right now. It needs non-residential premises, which an industrial lease can't fund at current revenue.
   - **Loan licence (COS-9):** turned down because the ingredients and process are custom and there's a risk the manufacturer copies the line.
   - **Effect:** stockists, marketplaces and corporate gifting are effectively closed. That makes **direct sales, stalls and classes the only channels**, and it lowers how big the business can get while it stays home-made.
   - **GST:** production is in Goa and almost every order ships to another state (mostly Karnataka, Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu). Selling goods across state lines requires GST registration **whatever the turnover**, so this applies to nearly all revenue, not a part of it. Two things make it lighter than it sounds:
     - toilet soap has been taxed at 5% since 22 Sep 2025
     - the quarterly filing scheme (QRMP) means returns every quarter, not every month

     The catch is cost against profit: CA fees of about ₹6–18k a year are a big share of about ₹28k a year in profit. Get a quote, and ask about filing low-value quarterly returns yourself.
   - **Address proof is the real GST blocker.** Under CBIC Instruction 03/2025-GST, a rented home needs one of these:
     - the landlord's ownership document
     - a consent letter from the landlord
     - a utility bill in your own name

     With a no-commercial-use rent agreement and an uncooperative landlord, there's no clean route from the Goa rental. A virtual office is the paid workaround but expensive against current profit. Ask a CA whether 18% GST on rent under reverse charge (tenant pays the tax instead of the landlord) would apply once registered.
   - **Zoning risk (new, Sep 2026):** in *Loganathan v. State of Tamil Nadu* (22 Sep 2026, applies nationwide), the Supreme Court told authorities to seal commercial premises in areas zoned and approved only for residential use. Where land use isn't clearly defined, the status quo holds. Goa's Aug 2026 building-regulation amendment allows limited professional offices in homes, but doesn't clearly allow manufacturing.
     - GST registration doesn't trigger zoning checks, but the GST address is publicly searchable and is a record of business activity at the address. The rent agreement's no-commercial-use clause is a separate risk with the landlord.
     - **Actions:**
       - Find out the flat's zoning under the Goa regional plan or outline development plan (S1–S4 residential, or mixed-use).
       - Follow the next hearing on 23 Nov 2026.
       - Keep the operation low-profile: no signage, no walk-in sales from home.
   - **Trade licence:** skip for now. GST doesn't need it, and it runs into the same residential-zoning question.
   - **Bonus:** a GST certificate plus Udyam should be enough for a current account, since banks ask a sole proprietor for two proofs of business.
   - **"Cottage industry" status:** not a legal exemption. The cosmetic licence and the interstate GST rule still apply. Two places it may help:
     - **Zoning:** if Goa's building regulations allow "household industries" in residential zones. Unconfirmed; ask Goa's Town and Country Planning department or an architect.
     - **Funding:** soap is on KVIC's list of village industries, so PMEGP (a scheme that subsidises a bank loan, roughly 15–35% of project cost) could help fund a compliant unit. Confirm current terms with KVIC or the Goa Khadi and Village Industries Board.
   - **What this means for the plan:** making soap at home has a weak legal footing, and it gets weaker the more visible the business becomes. GST address, cosmetic licence and zoning **all come back to one fix: non-residential premises.** Compliant premises (an industrial-estate unit or a shared facility) are the real cost of reaching a full-time income. Get two numbers now: the rent of a small Goa industrial-estate unit, and what PMEGP would fund. Together they set the revenue target for going formal.
5. **Most of the range is melt-and-pour.** Glycerin and goat-milk bases are bought ready-made, which makes them easy to copy. Moving to cold process makes you more distinctive, but bars need a 4–6 week cure. That ties up cash and adds delay, which will make the catching-up feeling worse unless the range is cut first.

## How other solo makers succeed (research)
- On soapmaking forums, the common view is that it **takes years** to build a steady customer base. Some sellers still show no profit after 2 years. Those who **make a living** add wholesale, classes, markets or selling supplies; direct sales of bars alone is rarely enough.
- The Sass Bar (Mumbai, 2019, started with ₹25k) reached about 3,000 soaps a month after several years, with gift boxes from ₹1,500.
- Indian market: ordinary handmade bars sell for ₹80–150. You're priced at the premium end, so gifting and premium positioning is the right lane.

## Verdict
- **As a full-time income in 12–18 months, at today's hours, channels and capacity: not viable.**
  - ₹75k take-home needs about **₹2 lakh a month in revenue**, roughly 25 times today.
  - That's about 180 orders or 700 bars a month, against capacity of under 100 bars.
  - That isn't a tweak. It's a different business: formal (licensed) premises, more sales channels, and someone working on it full-time.
- **As a business with a real product: yes, there's a signal.** The margin is good, the reorder rate is good, and premium prices are accepted. What's missing is **a way to reach new buyers, and the hours**, not product-market fit.
- **Honestly, today it's a well-run hobby that pays for itself.** Whether it becomes a business depends on choices below, not on luck.

## Direction and milestones
**Phase 0: Diwali push (now to 8 Nov 2026)**
- Buy the materials (under ₹5k is fine), but only for the top 8–10 products plus the discovery and gift boxes.
- Freeze until December: new products, ads, equipment, courses, CP expansion and new SoapLedger features.
- Stall test next week:
  - Covering the ₹2,500 fee alone needs about ₹4.2k in sales.
  - **Worth repeating only if sales reach ₹7k or more**, or if you collect 20+ WhatsApp contacts.
- Record a source on every order.
- **Target: ₹25k revenue from 1 Oct to 8 Nov.**

**Phase 1: Focus and sort out GST (Dec 2026 to Feb 2027)**
- Cut to about 10 products. Track actual hours every week.
- Get a CA quote for GST under the quarterly scheme (5% rate on soap).
- Pick **one** new way to reach buyers that doesn't need a licence and run it for 90 days:
  - a monthly stall circuit
  - wedding and return-gift orders from individuals
  - paid soap-making workshops, which sell your skill rather than the product
- **Milestone: ₹15k/month average revenue for Dec–Feb and 25% of customers reordering.**
- **Stop rule:** if revenue is still under ₹12k/month by end of Feb, keep it as a self-funding hobby and stop putting money and time into growth.

**Phase 2: Raise capacity, then decide (Mar to Jun 2027)**
- Reach ₹30k/month, which is the 100-bar ceiling.
- Raise capacity with more moulds and bigger batches, and make the chosen licence-free channel (stalls, gift orders or workshops) a steady ₹10k+/month.
- **Gate:** at ₹30k/month for 3 straight months, decide whether the maker goes full-time.

**Phase 3: Part-time salary (Jul to Dec 2027)**
- ₹60–80k/month revenue, so the maker takes home about ₹25–30k.

**Full-time income:** realistically **2–3 years out**. Formalising (licensed premises or a shared licensed facility) is a later milestone at about ₹75k–1 lakh/month of licence-free revenue, paid for out of profit. If that level is not reachable without a licence, the ceiling is a profitable side business, not a full-time income.
